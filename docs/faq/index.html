Frequently Asked Questions

How does Sovereign OS differ from edge AI initiatives by major hardware vendors?

Sovereign OS shares the architectural premise that personal context should be processed on the edge. Hardware-first vendors are economically incentivized to protect on-device data because their primary revenue is derived from hardware sales, allowing them to implement edge-centric AI within proprietary ecosystems. Sovereign OS provides an open-source, hardware-agnostic counterpart to this philosophy, ensuring that cryptographic data sovereignty is available as an auditable standard outside of closed ecosystems.

Why not utilize centralized cloud models for personal AI?

Centralized models require the continuous extraction of high-fidelity personal context to third-party data centers. Organizations whose valuations depend on data aggregation and algorithmic advertising are structurally incentivized to maintain this extraction model. Sovereign OS mitigates the risks associated with this topology by enforcing a strict edge boundary, ensuring the intelligence engine serves the individual rather than a centralized data repository.

What are the hardware requirements for Sovereign OS?

Maintaining a persistent, local memory graph that orchestrates multiple models is computationally intensive. Currently, a fully realized edge-only architecture sits just beyond the reach of standard consumer devices, requiring high-end hardware (e.g., 64GB+ unified memory) and heavily optimized foundational models. However, this hardware barrier is temporary; silicon continues to grow exponentially more power-efficient and cost-effective every year. Sovereign OS is built to be a universal, hardware-agnostic architecture. Whether deployed on advanced proprietary hardware like Apple Silicon or across open ecosystems like Linux and Windows, it establishes the structural standard today for a sovereign reality that is right around the corner.

How does Sovereign OS relate to the Human Context Protocol (HCP)?

The Human Context Protocol (HCP) is an active research program and proposed specification for decentralized memory. Sovereign OS serves as a structural implementation of HCP principles, enforcing a strict edge-only topology for the localized knowledge graph generated by the protocol.
